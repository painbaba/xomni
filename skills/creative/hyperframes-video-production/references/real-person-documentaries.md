# Documentaries about real people — legal-care rules

Proven on a 33-minute documentary about a living IAS officer and an ongoing
enforcement campaign + litigation. These rules kept every claim defensible.

## Sourcing rules (enforce on research subagents)

- **Named mainstream publications only**: Indian Express, Times of India,
  India Today, Hindustan Times, NDTV, ThePrint, Moneycontrol, CNBC TV18, PTI,
  Reuters, BBC, WHO/FSSAI primary docs. Never blogs/Twitter/X as fact sources.
- Each fact carries **inline source URL + date**. Headline + outlet + date
  (with a Google News search locator) is acceptable when canonical URLs are
  paywalled/JS-walled.
- **UNVERIFIED tag** for any single-source claim (e.g. one outlet reporting a
  rank/stat) — then either drop it or frame it as "as reported by X".
- Separate **allegations vs established facts** explicitly in the notes.
  Clean chits are established facts; "under inquiry" is not resolved.
- Subagents hit iteration caps and lose their final summaries — require them
  to **save notes to a .md file on disk** as they go (parent reads the file).

## Script framing rules

- On-screen attribution for every number and quote: "As reported by The
  Indian Express, July 2026" / "— The Times of India, as reported".
- Use "allegedly" for any wrongdoing not established in court.
- No dramatized accusations, no invented dialogue, no editorializing about
  guilt. Describe what was REPORTED, and by whom.
- When a person's reputation is contested, give both sides of the ledger a
  beat (supporters' quotes AND critics' quotes, with named sources).
- End credits name every outlet the documentary draws from.
- A closing line that separates fact from opinion: "Allegations remain
  allegations, and clean chits remain clean chits."

## Why

A documentary about a living person can be defamatory even when true if the
claim is unverifiable or the framing implies guilt. The "as reported" +
attribution pattern converts editorial risk into journalism practice — the
video states its sources on screen and never asserts more than they do.
