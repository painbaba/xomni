## Hook patterns (0:00–0:30)
- Five proven hook types: Question, Result, Cliffhanger, Social Proof, Visual Teaser; educational
  content → Question/Result hooks (TubeAnalytics).
- First 30s = "qualification round": early bails → algorithm stops recommending; 70% pass rate at
  30s gets the video tested wider (https://umbrellacreators.com/blog/the-30-second-rule-engineering-youtube-hooks).
- Hook-deliver cycle (hook → payoff → next hook) flattens the curve; open loops ("the third factor
  is the one that doubled…") carry viewers; cold open buys the 30s; no logo/"welcome back"
  (vidIQ tactics section).

## Pacing / visual change
- One idea per 2–3s; a 6s screen with one idea drags. First shot of a scene = longest (orient 4–5s).
- Variation is the engagement mechanism (dopamine/novelty): slow = emotional weight, fast = tension;
  constant same-pace = dull (https://www.insidetheedit.com/blog/pacing-in-video-editing).
- Doc-specific: quick cuts = urgency, longer shots = reflection; music/sound = emotional cues
  (https://www.inthewarroom.com/mastering-pacing-and-tension-in-documentaries/).
- ASL benchmarks: Kubrick 2001 = 13s; Bourne Supremacy = 2.4s; The Mist = 5.4s
  (https://www.filmmakersacademy.com/glossary/average-shot-length-of-films/, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shot_(filmmaking)).

## Sound design
- "People tolerate bad video, but they mute bad audio" — bad audio = instant abandonment
  (https://www.buttercut.ai/blog/video-sound-design-engagement-retention).
- Place tension cues (risers/booms/stingers) at scene/chapter boundaries and pre-reveals — the only
  placement where research shows music improving retention is the pre-roll moment (ERIC/JEO).
- Constant music under narration = "extraneous/seductive detail" (Mayer 2021, in ERIC paper).

## Chapters & structure
- Official rules: first timestamp 00:00, ≥3 timestamps ascending, min 10s
  (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579).
- Chapters don't cause early exits: a chapter-jumper delivers more watch time than a 30s bouncer
  (vidIQ); chapter titles = searchable "key moments".
- 3-act (Syd Field 1978, https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/three-act-structure/) + cold open
  (grab attention ASAP, https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-a-cold-open-in-tv/).

## Checklist → pipeline mapping (HyperFrames scene kinds)
- Cold open scene 1: payoff/cliffhanger, no title card; value promise ≤15s; silent start (no music
  bed first ~5s, swell in behind first line).
- Visual change every 3–5s inside stat/data scenes (alternate stat card → chart anim → headline →
  quote); dividers get 4–5s orientation beat; pattern interrupt every 60–90s (scene-kind swap,
  music tonal shift, SFX sting).
- Every stat = animated count-up/chart (2–4s, en-IN grouping), visual lands before narrator
  finishes the sentence.
- B-roll/archival substitutes: 35–50% of runtime non-core screens; relevance gate (must illustrate
  the narration line); return to core typography screen for key points.
- Tension cues at chapter boundaries; tonal arc per act; keep mean ≈ −19 to −25 dB, narration ~17×
  bed RMS.
- Chapters: ≥3, 00:00 first, aligned to visual dividers, titled as searchable key moments.
- Post-publish: check 15s + 30s marks on retention graph; replay own spikes; keep docs ≤ ~25 min
  (−11% engagement past 30 min).
