# India Unclaimed-Money / Claims-Assistance — LEGAL & REGULATORY Knowledge Bank (Aug 8, 2026)

Verified legal/regulatory side of the India claims-assistance project (complements `india-unclaimed-money-market-2026.md`, which covers players/fees/scale). Every fact below was verified against a live source this session; URL list at the end. Where no live evidence exists, that is stated explicitly.

## 1. Life insurance death claim — documents & IRDAI timelines

**Documents (LIC official page, licindia.in/claims-settlement-requirements):**
- Claim Form A (Claimant's Statement); certified extract of Death Register / death certificate; original policy document; proof of age if not admitted; claimant KYC.
- **If the policy is NOT nominated (or assigned / under MWP Act): "evidence of title to the deceased's estate"** — i.e. Succession Certificate / Letters of Administration / probate, OR the insurer's indemnity route.
- Death within 3 years of risk/revival ("early claim"): extra Forms B, B1, B2, C, E + FIR, post-mortem & police report for accident/unnatural death.
- IRDAI Master Circular 2024: **no claim shall be rejected or closed for want of documents or delayed intimation**; insurer must state required documents in the policy and on its website.

**Timelines — IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests, 2024** (Ref IRDAI/PP&GR/CIR/MISC/117/9/2024, dated 5 Sep 2024):
- Death claim, no investigation: **15 days from intimation**. Death claim warranting investigation: **45 days from intimation**. Surrender/partial withdrawal: 7 days. Maturity/survival/annuity: on due date.
- Delay → **interest at bank rate + 2% p.a.** from intimation to payment, paid suo moto.
- Note: older 2017-era wording was "30 days from receipt of all documents"; the 2024 MC anchors on *intimation*.
- LIC settled 98.2% of death claims by count in FY 2024-25 (IRDAI).

**Escalation:** insurer grievance cell → **Insurance Ombudsman** (free, 12 centres; award binding on insurer — ₹50 lakh cap for personal lines; insurer must comply within 30 days of award else **₹5,000/day penalty** to complainant) → consumer forum / civil court.

## 2. Succession Certificate (SC)

- Granted **only by Civil/District Court** under Indian Succession Act 1925, ss.370-390 (petition under s.372). **No notary-issued succession certificate exists under Indian law** — the "notary route" people refer to = notarized legal-heir affidavits + indemnity bond accepted by insurers/banks for small claims instead of a court SC.
- Court fee: ad valorem (percentage of estate value), state-specific (e.g. Maharashtra ~3% under Maharashtra Court Fees Act; Karnataka/UP/WB ad valorem per their Acts).
- Timeline: **4-8 months uncontested; 1-3 years contested**. Process: petition + documents → notice/objection window → certificate. s.383 gives indemnity to institutions paying the certificate-holder in good faith.
- Legal Heir Certificate (revenue/tehsildar, ~15-30 days, nominal fee) is generally NOT accepted by insurers as title evidence.
- **LIC Form 3805 Indemnity Agreement** is the documented no-SC route: LIC pays legal heirs against an indemnity bond (executed on stamp paper, notarized). **No uniform statutory rupee threshold found** — the cap is insurer-internal policy; do not assert a specific figure.

## 3. Nominee vs legal heir (life insurance)

- Insurance Act 1938 **s.39**: payment to the nominee fully discharges the insurer. The **2015 amendment** created "beneficial nominee" status for spouse/children/parents; other nominees hold as **trustee for legal heirs**.
- **2024-25 case law is split/unsettled**: SC *Shakti Yezdani* (2024), Karnataka HC (Feb 2025), Allahabad HC (May 2025): nominee is trustee; succession law prevails. Bombay HC (Jul 2025, *Ravindran* line): beneficial nominee has statutory ownership overriding succession law. Practical rule: insurer pays nominee and is discharged; heirs pursue the nominee in civil court.
- **No nomination**: claim payable to legal heirs under personal law (Hindu Succession Act 1956 etc.); heirs must prove title via SC/LA/probate or the indemnity route.
- *Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, 2025 (No. 40 of 2025)*, assented 20 Dec 2025, amends Insurance Act/LIC Act/IRDAI Act — gazette summary covers definitions/intermediaries; **no s.39/nominee change identified**.

## 4. Unclaimed amounts: SCWF, NOT IEPF (key correction for this domain)

- "Unclaimed Amount" = amount payable (incl. accrued income) unpaid >12 months from due date due to non-contactability (IRDAI MC 2024).
- **10 years unclaimed → transferred to Senior Citizens' Welfare Fund (SCWF)** by the insurer (Finance Act 2015 + IRDAI MC). Claimable **up to 25 years after SCWF transfer, through the insurer**; then **escheats to the Central Government**.
- Search: **Bima Bharosa unclaimed-amounts index** `https://bimabharosa.irdai.gov.in/Home/UnclaimedAmount` (central links to every insurer's list) + insurer sites (LIC public "Unclaimed Amounts of Policyholders" search at merchant.licindia.in .../UnclaimedPolicyDues). Identify a person by matching any 2 of: policy number, PAN, name, DOB.
- **IEPF (the 7-year rule) is for company dividends/shares under Companies Act 2013 s.124** — applies to LIC **shareholder** dividends (LIC listed May 2022) and other listed insurers; **NOT to policyholder money**. Don't conflate the two regimes.
- **IEPF claim process (Form IEPF-5):** register on MCA portal (mca.gov.in e-filing; PAN mandatory, OTP-verified) → get **Entitlement Letter** from the company's RTA first → file IEPF-5 with attachments (Aadhaar, PAN, Client Master List of demat account — shares refund in demat, dividends by bank transfer, proof of entitlement) → send physical docs (signed printout, acknowledgement, entitlement letter, **indemnity bond** + stamp-duty proof, cancelled cheque, death certs for deceased joint holders) to the company **Nodal Officer** → company sends e-verification report within **30 days** (Rule 7(3)) → Authority disposes within **60 days** of the complete verification report (Rule 7(6)); delay must be recorded and communicated. Practice: ~2-6 months.
- Cost: **no fee to claimants**; only stamp duty on the indemnity bond. IEPF Rules amended **6 Oct 2025** (G.S.R. 733(E)) — revised, streamlined IEPF-5. IEPFA officially urges claimants to **apply directly** (i.e. warning against intermediaries).

## 5. Third-party assistance & fees

- IRDAI regulates insurers/agents/brokers/TPAs; **no license category exists for claims-assistance companies** — a services firm doing paperwork/claim-filing is not insurance intermediation.
- **BCI Rules Part VI, Ch II, §II, Rule 20**: "An advocate shall not stipulate for a fee contingent on the results of litigation or agree to share the proceeds thereof" — binds **advocates only**. Non-lawyer firms may charge success fees for administrative claims assistance.
- Limits: (a) non-lawyers **cannot represent in court** (Advocates Act 1961 s.33; s.45 penalty) — SC petitions need an enrolled advocate, who cannot take a contingency fee; (b) **cannot buy the claim** — Transfer of Property Act s.6(e) bars transfer of a mere right to sue; (c) Consumer Protection Act 2019 applies to services — unconscionable fee terms challengeable; (d) MCA/IEPFA publicity against touts = reputational risk.

## 6. DPDP Act 2023 + cold outreach from public unclaimed lists

- DPDP Act 2023 (Act 22 of 2023): processing requires **consent** (ss.4-6) or a **legitimate use** under s.7. **The final Act DROPPED the publicly-available-data exemption that was in the 2022 Bill** — there is no general "it's public, so no consent needed" route (verified against the Act text from indiacode.nic.in).
- **s.17(1)(a)**: Chapter II obligations (consent/notice) don't apply where processing "is necessary for enforcing any legal right or claim" — the arguable hook for processing data *after* engagement to enforce a claim, not for mass cold outreach. Untested in this exact context.
- Deceased persons are not "Data Principals" (Act defines Data Principal as an individual) — the deceased's own data is likely outside the Act; but **contacted family members are living individuals** whose data is protected.
- **DPDP Rules 2025** (notified 14 Nov 2025, in force via 18-month phased compliance): separate consent notices, breach notification, rights responses within 90 days; penalties up to ₹250 crore (security safeguards), ₹200 crore (breach/children), ₹50 crore (other violations).
- Cold calls/SMS additionally governed by **TRAI TCCCPR 2018** (DND registry, telemarketer registration, commercial communications only with consent/preference, registered headers). Mass unsolicited phone/SMS outreach from public lists is high-risk on both DPDP (consent) and TRAI (DND) grounds; postal/email outreach is less regulated but DPDP still applies to living persons' data.

## Caveats / no-live-evidence items
- No uniform statutory rupee threshold for indemnity-bond settlement without SC (LIC Form 3805 practice confirmed; amount caps are insurer-internal).
- DPDP s.17(1)(a) claim-enforcement exemption untested in this exact context.
- "Notary succession certificate" is a myth in Indian law; notarized indemnity/affidavit is the real alternative.
